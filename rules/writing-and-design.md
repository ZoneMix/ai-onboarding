# Writing and design

## Prose

- **Write in a plain human voice,** the way a good essayist does: whole sentences and short
  paragraphs that carry the point along. Short and human are both the goal.
- **Avoid assistant tics:** stacks of bullets with bold lead-in labels, "Label: fragment"
  lines, tables where a sentence would do, and clipped summaries. Use a list when the content
  really is a list, and a table for reference material.
- **No em dashes or middots.** Rewrite the sentence instead of swapping the punctuation.

## Interface copy

- **Labels are short and plain.** Cut helper subtexts, captions and footnote sentences that
  don't carry information.
- **Buttons and navigation say what they do** in plain words, not icons or abbreviations
  alone.

## Page design

- **Quiet, not busy.** Separate things with space rather than rules and boxes. No accent bars
  down the left edge of callouts or cards; a plain surface panel is enough.
- **Two typefaces at most,** each with one clear job (headings and controls, reading text).
- **Product grade, not scaffold.** Design the whole experience (layout, hierarchy, feedback,
  empty and error states), then check it in a real browser at phone and desktop widths, in
  light and dark mode, before calling it done.
- **Accessible by default.** See `accessibility.md`.
